### About

I'm a computer science undergraduate interested in AI systems, computational music, and software that feels quiet and useful.

I like taking complicated things apart, finding the structure underneath, and putting them back together as something understandable. Lately, that has meant working on verifiable AI workflows, symbolic music generation, and local-first tools.

Outside the editor, there's usually a piano, some Chopin or Animenz, and a pixel-art world still under construction.

### Selected work

**[MCMer](https://github.com/qinghuan-yu/MCMer)**  
*A verification-first assistant for mathematical modeling.*  
Exploring structured model representations, reproducible computation, numerical checks, and traceable evidence. The current `main` branch focuses on trustworthy modeling guidance; an earlier multi-agent writing workflow is preserved in `agent`.

**[Dialograph](https://github.com/qinghuan-yu/Dialograph)**  
*Evidence-grounded analysis of conversations.*  
Turns voluntarily provided, local chat archives into structured timelines, interaction patterns, and evidence-backed reports. It is designed to preserve counter-evidence and uncertainty—not to turn a model's interpretation into a verdict.

**[Pianalysis](https://github.com/qinghuan-yu/Pianalysis)**  
*Melody-conditioned symbolic piano generation.*  
An ongoing experiment connecting MIDI preprocessing, melody/accompaniment annotation, and Transformer-based generation. The ambition is musical texture; the reality is still a lot of data cleaning and listening.

**[Minimalist Calendar](https://github.com/qinghuan-yu/Minimalist-Calendar)**  
*A local-first Markdown diary.*  
A small desktop app built around a simple idea: one day, one `.md` file. Plain-text ownership, restrained typography, and fewer things competing for attention.

### Side projects

- **[Decision Arena / deconstruct](https://github.com/qinghuan-yu/deconstruct)** — A multi-agent decision tool that makes different perspectives debate before producing a structured recommendation.
- **[After the Long Summer](https://github.com/qinghuan-yu/After-the-Long-Summer)** — An early-stage pixel-art game in Godot. Mostly an excuse to study atmosphere, lighting, and the feeling of a small place.

### Currently exploring

- How to make AI-generated results easier to verify, reproduce, and question.
- How melody, harmony, and piano texture can be represented in a learnable form.
- The computer systems underneath the abstractions: architecture, networks, and everything in between.
